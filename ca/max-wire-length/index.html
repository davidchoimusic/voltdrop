How we calculated this

Why 3% and 5%?

Wire has resistance, so some voltage is lost as heat before it reaches your device. Lose too much and motors run hot, LEDs dim or flicker, chargers underperform, and breakers can trip for no obvious reason.

CEC Rule 8-102 sets a mandatory maximum: keep the voltage drop on a branch circuit or feeder at or under 3%, and the combined drop from the service to the farthest load at or under 5%. These are enforceable limits in Canada, not optional performance guidelines. Low-voltage systems like 12 V solar often need an even tighter design target because 3% of 12 V hurts much more than 3% of 240 V.

Why don't other calculators agree with each other?

Almost always one of two things: some tools want round-trip length where others want one-way (we always use one-way and double it for you), and some use slightly different resistance values for the wire. Open "How we calculated this" above and you can check our numbers against anything.
