🔌 What Size Wire for a 60 Amp Breaker in Canada?

🇺🇸 US version

The short answer

Under the conservative 60°C termination method our Canadian tables use, start with #4 copper NMD90 for 60 A; check the exact installation in the Ampacity Check tool before relying on a marked 75°C termination and #6 copper at 65 A.

The verified 60°C column gives #6 copper only 55 A, so that method selects #4 copper at 70 A. The verified 75°C column gives #6 copper 65 A. CEC Rule 4-006 and the equipment markings decide which termination limit applies. Ampacity Check tool.

The #6-vs-#4 divide

The #6-versus-#4 split comes from the termination column. #6 copper is 55 A in the verified 60°C column and 65 A in the verified 75°C column; the conservative method our Canadian tables use therefore starts at #4 copper for 60 A unless the installation supports the 75°C column.

Common Canadian 60-amp jobs

In the CEC Rule 8-104 rated-equipment case, a 60 A conductor limit supports 48 A continuous because 60 A × 80% = 48 A. Other common jobs include sub-panel feeds and larger heat-pump or mini-split circuits; follow the equipment nameplate.

Worked example: 60 A at 240 V over 46 m (150 ft)

Our verified calculator selects #4 copper (41,740 circular mils): 2 × 12.9 × 60 × 150 ÷ 41,740 = 5.56 V, or 2.32% of 240 V. The result passes ampacity and the mandatory 3% drop limit.

60-amp wire size FAQ

Can I run an EV charger on a 60 amp circuit in Canada?
In the CEC Rule 8-104 rated-equipment case, a 60 A conductor limit supports 48 A continuous: 60 A × 80% = 48 A. Confirm the charger's setting, equipment markings, and local requirements.
Why do people say both 6 and 4 gauge for 60 amps in Canada?
The termination column changes the result. #6 copper is 65 A at 75°C, but only 55 A at 60°C; the 60°C method therefore selects #4 copper at 70 A.
What about a 60 amp sub-panel in Canada?
