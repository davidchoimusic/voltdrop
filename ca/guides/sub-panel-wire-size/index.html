🏠 Wire Size for a Sub-Panel in Canada (Shed or Detached Garage)

Sized to the Canadian Electrical Code — where voltage drop isn't a suggestion, it's Rule 8-102. 🇺🇸 US version

The short answer

For a 100 amp sub-panel at 45 m (150 ft): #2 copper or 1/0 aluminum. For a 60 amp sub-panel at 30 m (100 ft): #6 copper or #4 aluminum. Full table below — and unlike in the US, staying inside the drop limit is mandatory in Canada: CEC Rule 8-102 caps voltage drop at 3% on a branch circuit, 3% on the feeder, 5% total, as enforceable code. Your exact run: Wire Size calculator.

The two checks (and why Canada makes the second one law)

Sizing a feeder is two stacked checks. First, ampacity — the wire must be rated for the current under CEC Table 2 or Table 4, with the applicable termination, derating, and overcurrent-device rules. Second, voltage drop — over a long run to a shed or garage, the wire must be fat enough that the far panel still sees healthy voltage. In the US that second check is a recommendation; in Canada, Rule 8-102 makes it a "shall." Past roughly 30 m, it's the drop check — not ampacity — that sets your wire size, and an inspector can hold you to it.

Worked example: 100 A at 46 m

Drop = 2 × K × amps × one-way feet ÷ circular mils. #2 copper (66,360 cmil) at 150 ft: 2 × 12.9 × 100 × 150 ÷ 66,360 = 5.8 V = 2.4% of 240 V — inside the mandatory 3%. One size down (#3) gives 3.1% — over the line, and in Canada that's a code issue, not a judgment call. Check any run in the Voltage Drop calculator.

Canadian specifics people miss

1. It's a 4-wire feeder — two hots, neutral, and a separate bonding conductor, with neutral and bond on separate bars at the sub-panel. 2. The cable will likely be NMD90, TECK90, or direct-burial (NMWU) / conduit — Canadian names for what Americans call Romex, armored cable, and UF; underground runs to outbuildings are commonly NMWU at proper burial depth or conductors in buried conduit. 3. The drop budget is structured: 3% for the feeder, 3% for the branch beyond it, 5% total — drop doesn't "reset" at the sub-panel. 4. Bury copper once — if the garage might someday charge an EV or run a welder, upsizing now is the cheapest it will ever be.
