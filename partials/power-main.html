How we calculated this

Watts vs kW vs kVA — the 30-second version

Watts (W) is real usable power; 1 kW = 1,000 W. kVA is the "apparent" power the wiring actually has to carry. For heaters and simple loads they're equal. For motors and electronics, some current sloshes back and forth without doing useful work — that's why kVA can be higher than kW, and the ratio between them is the power factor.

The formulas we use

DC: watts = volts × amps. Single-phase AC: watts = volts × amps × power factor. Three-phase AC: watts = √3 (1.732) × line-to-line volts × amps × power factor. Every result shows its math — open "How we calculated this."
