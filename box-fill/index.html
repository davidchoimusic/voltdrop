📦 Box Fill

Is your junction or device box big enough? Count what's in it, get a clear fits / doesn't-fit answer.

cu in

Plastic boxes have their volume stamped inside (e.g. "18.0 CU IN") — pick Custom and type it. Metal box sizes use the standard code volumes.

Most homes: 14 AWG (15 A circuits) or 12 AWG (20 A circuits). Mixed sizes coming — for now pick the size you have most of (or the largest, to be safe).

wires

Count every hot and neutral that enters the box — including any that pass straight through unbroken (those count too). Don't count ground wires here (next question), and don't count pigtails that start and end inside the box.

devices

Switches, receptacles/outlets, dimmers. Each one counts as two wires' worth of space.

All the grounds together count as one wire's worth of space (that's the code rule — not one each).

Clamps built inside the box (common in metal boxes) count as one wire's worth. Connectors that stay outside the box don't count.

Why box fill matters

Cram too many wires into a box and connections get stressed, insulation gets nicked, and heat builds up — a classic cause of failed inspections and flickering circuits. The U.S. electrical code (NEC 314.16) assigns every wire a space allowance based on its size, and the box must have at least that much room.

The counting rules, in plain English

Each hot or neutral entering the box = 1 count — including wires that pass straight through unbroken (those count once too). Each device (switch/outlet) = 2 counts. All ground wires together = 1 count (with more than four grounds, each extra adds a ¼ count under the 2020 rules). Built-in cable clamps = 1 count. Pigtails that live entirely inside the box are free. Multiply the counts by your wire size's space allowance and compare to the box volume — we do exactly that, and show the math.
